Understanding the scale

What does the AQI
number mean for you?

0–50
Good
Air quality is satisfactory. Go outdoors freely.
51–100
Moderate
Acceptable. Sensitive groups should limit prolonged exertion.
101–150
Sensitive
Unhealthy for children, elderly, and people with lung disease.
151–200
Unhealthy
Everyone may begin to experience health effects. Reduce outdoor activity.
201–300
Very Unhealthy
Health alert. Avoid outdoor exertion.
301+
Hazardous
Emergency conditions. Stay indoors.
What we're doing

Communication & action
for liveable cities.

01
📡
Real-time Data Broadcasting

AQI and environmental sensor data published openly every hour across mobile apps, roadside digital boards, and the public data API — so no Nepali has to guess the air quality in their city.

Access the API →
02
🏫
School Integration Programme

Sustainability curriculum embedded in 2,400 schools across all seven provinces. By 2027, 450,000 students will graduate with structured environmental literacy.

Read the curriculum →
03
🌿
Urban Greening Mandate

Municipal mandate requiring all new construction above 200 m² to include green roofing, permeable surfaces, or street-level planting — tracked through this dashboard in real time.

Policy details →
04
🤝
Community Town Halls

Monthly open forums in eight major cities where residents meet city planners, environmental scientists, and local government to review data and set air quality improvement targets together.

Upcoming events →
05
🚴
Clean Mobility Corridors

Dedicated cycling and e-mobility lanes being constructed in Kathmandu, Pokhara, and Lalitpur — reducing vehicle emissions on the highest-traffic roads by an estimated 18%.

Track progress →
06
🏢
Corporate Accountability

120+ companies enrolled in the Nepal Urban ESG Compact, publicly committing to emissions targets and contributing to the city greening fund. Annual progress reports published here.

View partners →
